# Project vision

## One-sentence vision

Build a mobile-first, side-view combat RPG where every turn is a readable
tactical choice, every profession has a distinct identity, and career and
equipment choices visibly shape the fighter.

## Player promise

The player creates a combatant, chooses a profession and loadout, studies an
opponent's intent, and wins compact duels through timing, resource management,
and build synergy. Victories grow the character's career and unlock new ways to
approach the next fight.

## Audience and platform

- **Primary platform:** phones and tablets, with touch controls as the baseline.
- **Secondary target:** desktop builds for development and playtesting.
- **Session shape:** a fight should be understandable at a glance and finish in
  a few minutes; menus and progression can be explored between fights.
- **Player profile:** fans of tactical turn-based combat, dark-fantasy
  archetypes, and meaningful long-term character development.

## Design pillars

### 1. Tactics are legible

Show whose turn it is, what threats are coming, what an action costs, and why an
outcome occurred. Depth should come from decisions and interactions, not hidden
rules or unreadable numbers.

### 2. Each turn changes the situation

Attacks, defenses, statuses, positioning in the side-view lane, and limited
resources create trade-offs. A strong turn may set up a later payoff rather than
simply maximize immediate damage.

### 3. Careers define a fighter

Professions provide a clear starting identity and a path of advances. The
player can specialize without being locked into a single rigid class or a
single optimal build.

### 4. Combat has weight

Impact, interruption, recovery, and defeat should feel consequential. Feedback
supports the rules: it never obscures critical information or changes a
resolved result.

### 5. Mobile-first, not mobile-only

Touch targets, one-hand reach, short decision flows, scalable text, and
responsive layouts are first-class requirements. Desktop benefits from the same
clear hierarchy.

## Experience principles

- Telegraph dangerous enemy actions before they resolve.
- Prefer a small number of distinctive resources and statuses over layers of
  redundant modifiers.
- Let equipment and career choices create combinations, not runaway stat
  inflation.
- Explain probability and outcomes in plain language.
- Keep accessibility options available for animation intensity, text scale,
  contrast, and combat speed.

## Scope boundaries

### Initial target

- Single-player, local progression
- One-on-one, turn-based duels in a 2D side-view presentation
- Five starting professions, a small skill roster, and reusable enemy
  archetypes
- A vertical slice with one arena, regular encounters, and a boss

### Not required for the first slice

- Multiplayer, matchmaking, or competitive ranking
- Online accounts, cloud saves, live operations, or monetization
- Tactical grid movement or party combat
- Procedural campaigns or an expansive narrative campaign

## Success criteria

The first slice succeeds if a new player can understand an enemy's intent,
choose and resolve an action without confusion, and identify how their
profession and equipment affected the result. A repeat player should be able
to make a meaningfully different build and use a different tactical approach.
