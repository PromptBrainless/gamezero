# Game Director Pitch

## The promise

GameZero should feel like a hard decision made in a quiet moment.
The battlefield is small enough to read, but every choice has weight.
Victory is not a blur of effects: it is the moment the player understands why
a defense held, a threat broke, or a carefully chosen action changed the fight.

This is creative direction supporting the [Vision Manifest](vision-manifest.md),
not a claim that the intended experience is already implemented.

## The fantasy

You are not commanding an endless army or collecting a thousand interchangeable
heroes. You choose a career and learn its discipline. A mercenary, duelist, or
scholar mage should offer a recognizable way to face danger through the
existing approved content, not a promise of unbounded class systems.

Progress should feel earned: a sequence of encounters in which understanding
matters, resources have meaning, and the next decision carries the memory of
the last. The exact carryover policies still require gameplay approval.

## The world

Grounded fantasy, worn by conflict. A road is no longer safe. A military title
does not guarantee honor. A ritual threat does not need an enormous exposition
to make an encounter unsettling.

The current German content gives us concrete encounter identities to work from.
Build atmosphere around that material; do not invent regions, factions, quests,
or lore databases merely to satisfy this pitch.

## What the player does

Choose a career. Read the next encounter. Commit to an action with a clear cost.
Watch the result unfold in an understandable order. Receive the reward, review
progress, and continue the run.

That [first-playable loop](vision.md) is the experience to finish first.
The interface should help the player ask useful questions: What can I do?
Who can I affect? What matters now? What changed?
It must answer from real rules and state, not decorative promises.

## Combat feel

Concentrated, deliberate, readable. Give each approved action a clear identity
and each state change a place in the event sequence. Feedback can be satisfying
without obscuring the numbers or stealing the player's time.

On a phone, text and touch targets must remain usable. On a wider screen, space
should improve comprehension rather than invite clutter. Device orientation,
final art, audio, and animation scope remain subject to their own production gates.

## What makes it different

The ambition is precision rather than abundance. A compact roster can become
deep when its interactions are distinct, its rules are reproducible, and its
presentation teaches instead of concealing.

The [architecture](target-architecture.md) protects that experience:
canonical content, deterministic combat, and a host that presents rather than
rewrites outcomes. Good tools and validation serve the player's trust.

## The long-term dream

Once the slice works, more encounters and identities can extend its language.
Better authoring and simulation tools can make that growth deliberate.
Additional platforms can consume the same clean boundaries.
These are possibilities, not commitments to systems absent from the current build.

## Creative guardrails

- Use atmosphere to sharpen decisions, not replace them.
- Prefer a few memorable, readable interactions over speculative mechanic breadth.
- Do not add gameplay to justify an icon, effect, scene, or marketing phrase.
- Respect the [schema](data-schema.md), [slice scope](vertical-slice-implementation-plan.md),
  and [art review gates](art-sprint-1-production-pack.md).
- Describe aspirations as aspirations; judge delivered experience with actual
  playability evidence, not a pitch or screenshot alone.
